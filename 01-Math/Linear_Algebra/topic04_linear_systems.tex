\subsection*{Summary}
One of the core uses of matrices is to solve systems of equations. This reduces to finding the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ such that $A\vec{x} = \vec{v}$.

\subsection{System of Equations}

One of the uses of matrices is to \textbf{solve systems of equations}.

\paragraph{Example:}
\[
6x - 3y + 2z = 7
\]
\[
x + 2y + 5z = 0
\]
\[
2x - 8y - z = -2
\]

Which is:
\[
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 2 & -8 & -1 \end{bmatrix}}_{A}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}}_{\vec{x}}
=
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 0 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}}_{\vec{v}}
\]

So now we have:
\[
A\vec{x} = \vec{v}
\]

\subsection{Inverse Matrix --- $A^{-1}$}

\begin{defbox}
$A^{-1}$ is a unique transformation with the property that if you first apply $A$ then $A^{-1}$, you end up right where you started. $(A A^{-1})$ --- this is called the \textbf{identity transformation}.
\end{defbox}

\[
A^{-1}A\vec{x} = A^{-1}\vec{v} \Rightarrow \text{The ``do nothing'' matrix}
\]

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item If $\det(A) \neq 0 \Rightarrow A^{-1}$ exists
  \item If $\det(A) = 0 \Rightarrow$ No $A^{-1}$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rank}

\begin{defbox}
When the output of a transformation is a \textbf{line}, the transformation has a \textbf{rank of one}.
\end{defbox}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item For a two-dimensional plane $\rightarrow$ rank of two
  \item The rank = the \textbf{number of dimensions in the output} of the transformation (column space)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Column Space}

\begin{defbox}
The set of \textbf{all possible outputs} of the matrix --- it's called the column space.
\end{defbox}

\subsection{Null Space or ``Kernel''}

\begin{defbox}
The space of \textbf{all vectors that become null} (zero vector) after the transformation.
\end{defbox}
